# Spotify Part Queue

Some songs are split into several tracks, like Lorna Shore's *Pain Remains I, II, III.* When you play them from a shuffled playlist or mix, Spotify jumps to a random song after part 1. But this app instead of jumping to a random song, automatically queues the next part. Once there are no more parts left, it jumps back to the playlist and resumes the shuffle.

## What it does
- **Next part exists:** in the last 10 seconds of a song, it adds the next part to your queue, so the transition is gapless.
- **Chains:** it repeats this for part 3, part 4, and so on.
- **Chain ends:** when there is no next part (or it isn't in your playlist), it jumps to a random song in your playlist and turns shuffle back on, so your playlist carries on.
- **System tray:** minimize the window and it hides in the system tray. Click the tray icon to bring it back, or right-click it for Start / Stop and Quit.

It finds parts in two ways:
1. **Automatically**, from titles like `I`, `II`, `Pt. 2` or `Part 3` on the same album.
2. **From `links.json`**, for songs that flow into each other without numbers in the title.

## Requirements
- **Spotify Premium**. Spotify requires it for playback control through its API, and for the account that owns the developer app.
- A free Spotify developer app (guide below). No client secret is needed.

## Setup (one time, about 2 minutes)
The app has a **Setup guide** button that walks you through this, with buttons that open the dashboard and copy the redirect URI.

1. Go to the [Spotify Developer Dashboard](https://developer.spotify.com/dashboard) and click **Create app**.
2. Add this exact **Redirect URI**: `http://127.0.0.1:8888/callback`
3. Tick **Web API** and save.
4. Open the app's **Settings** and copy the **Client ID**.

Every user needs their own Spotify app. In Development Mode, Spotify limits each app to 5 authorized accounts, so one shared app can't serve everyone.

## Run the app (.exe)
1. Download `SpotifyPartQueue.exe` from the [Releases page](https://github.com/Zeoxxionn/spotify-part-queue/releases).
2. Put it in its own folder and double-click it. Windows may show a SmartScreen warning because the exe is unsigned. Click **More info → Run anyway**.
3. Paste your Client ID and click **Start**.
4. The first time, a browser window asks you to log in to Spotify and agree.
5. Play a shuffled playlist in Spotify and leave the app open (minimize it to the tray if you like).

## Adding songs without part numbers
Open `links.json` (or click **Edit links.json** in the app) and add an entry:

```json
[
  { "artist": "Fleshgod Apocalypse", "from": "The Deceit", "to": "The Violation" }
]
```

Artist and song names must match Spotify's spelling (capitals don't matter). Both songs must be on the same album. Pull requests with more links are welcome.

## Known limitations
- Skipping a song in its last 10 seconds leaves the already-queued next part in your queue. Spotify's API can't remove queue items.
- The random jump at the end of a chain is a fresh playback request, so there's a short pause, and the song it picks may be one you've already heard.
- For Spotify-made mixes (like Daily Mix), the app can't read the playlist contents, so it can't check whether the next part is in the mix. It queues the next part anyway.
- The app only works while it's running.

## Build the exe yourself (for developers)
The icon files are generated by `make_icon.py` (or replace `icon.png` with your own and convert it to `icon.ico`).

```
pip install pyinstaller
python make_icon.py
pyinstaller --onefile --windowed --name SpotifyPartQueue --icon icon.ico --add-data "icon.ico;." --hidden-import pystray._win32 main.py
```

The result is in `dist/`.

## Privacy
Your Client ID is saved in `.env` and your login token in `.cache-pkce`, both next to the app on your own computer. The app uses Spotify's PKCE login, so there is no client secret anywhere. Don't share or upload the `.cache-pkce` file.
